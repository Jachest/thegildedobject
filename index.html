      The case for recognition

      Recognition costs little. Losing good people costs a great deal.

      If you manage people, you already know who carried the year. This page sets out what the research says happens when you tell them so, and what it says about how to do it well.

      Retention

      People who are recognized well are far less likely to leave.

      45%

      Well-recognized employees were 45% less likely to have left their organization two years later.

      Gallup and Workhuman tracked nearly 3,500 employees from 2022 to 2024. Gallup, September 2024

      22%

      Just 22% of employees say they get the right amount of recognition for the work they do.

      Same Gallup study, 2024 data.

      200%

      Gallup estimates that replacing a leader or manager costs about 200% of salary. A technical role costs about 80%, a frontline role about 40%.

      Gallup's replacement cost estimates, same article.

      The award itself

      A symbol changes how the moment is remembered.

      12%

      In a field experiment, workers offered a purely symbolic award, with no money attached, outperformed the control group by about 12% on average.

      Kosfeld and Neckermann, "Getting More Work for Nothing? Symbolic Awards and Worker Performance", American Economic Journal: Microeconomics, 2011

      3×

      Employees recognized with a symbolic award are three times more likely to remember the occasion. Generic trophies and impersonal plaques do not have nearly the same effect.

      O.C. Tanner, 2023 Global Culture Report

      Performance and culture

      Recognition changes the people who see it, too.

      7.3%

      Students doing a three-hour data-entry job sat in groups of eight. When the top three in a group were given thank-you cards, the group's output rose 7.3% in the final hour. Most of the gain came from people who did not get a card.

      Bradler, Dur, Neckermann and Non, "Employee Recognition and Performance: A Field Experiment". IZA summary

      4.9×

      Employees who say their recognition program is aligned with their organization's values are 4.9 times as likely to strongly agree that they know what is expected of them at work.

      Gallup and Workhuman, September 2023

      Reading the numbers fairly

      What the research does and does not show.

      • It is about recognitionThe evidence is for recognizing people well. An award is the part of that moment they can keep. It does not replace saying what they did and why it mattered.
      • Association, not proofThe Gallup figures come from surveys. They show that well-recognized people leave less often. They do not prove that recognition alone is the cause.
      • Who paid for itWorkhuman, Gallup's research partner, and O.C. Tanner both sell recognition programs. O.C. Tanner does not publish a sample size or method for its figures here.
      • Small experimentsIn both field experiments the award was a card, given to students hired for a short data-entry project. The 7.3% gain was measured over one hour.
      • One award is not a programThe retention figure describes people's whole experience of recognition at work. How much a single award adds is not known.
      • Presentation mattersO.C. Tanner's own data says an award handed over without a proper moment of recognition can do harm.

      Doing it well

      Three things the research agrees on.

      • Make it personalName the person and the specific achievement. An award that could have gone to anyone reads that way.
      • Present it in personGive it in front of the people they work with, and say what they did.
      • Tie it to your companyYour name and mark on the piece connect the achievement to the organization it served.

      The studio

      To give great work a lasting form.

      We design and make awards in acrylic and leather, in St. Petersburg, Florida, so that recognition is something a person can hold and keep.

      Our vision

      Every person who does exceptional work has something on their desk that says so.

      Materials

      Three materials, chosen for how they hold light.

      • AcrylicEngraving frosts clear acrylic, so your mark and the recipient's name read white against the light and never wear off.
      • LeatherEach leather shape is cut to fit its piece and bonded by hand. It gives the award warmth and something to touch.
      • HardwareMetal standoffs lift the acrylic off its backing, so light passes behind the engraving.
